# Questions people ask

Answer in three sentences or fewer. Use this review's own results where they apply.

**Are these Sasol's own records?**
No. The review covers the files in this project's folder, which aren't Sasol's own records, so nothing here speaks to Sasol's own payments.

**How much of the business does this cover?**
Only the agreements and the payments in the project folder, over the period the payment history covers, in rand excluding VAT. The dashboard's overview gives the counts, the value and the period. The figures are not group totals.

**How do you know the terms were read correctly?**
Each term is quoted in full from its clause, and an independent check reads every term a second way. Where the two disagree, or an agreement can be read two ways, a person decides.

**Why did it stop?**
Some agreements can each be read two ways, and the data can't say which reading applies. Calculating first would mean guessing, so the review shows what each reading means and asks the person accountable.

**What if a different choice had been made?**
Each decision page shows both readings side by side: how many payments would be early or late, and what that costs. The review can be run again in a new chat with the other choice.

**Who can make these decisions, and who can approve the release?**
Agreement terms are decided by a Contracts Manager, Category Manager, Procurement Manager or Legal Counsel, with their full name and a reason. The release is approved by a different person, a Financial Controller, Group Treasurer, Financial Manager or Chief Financial Officer, who confirms they reviewed the findings, the decisions and the recommendations.

**Why can paying a small supplier early be right?**
Paying a qualifying small enterprise sooner, within the period its agreement names, can be part of an enterprise and supplier development commitment, so it's intended, not leakage. Where a person decided the commitment governs, those payments aren't findings.

**Why aren't the discounts taken counted as savings?**
Those payments were made inside the discount window, which is exactly right, so they're good practice, not findings.

**What does "paid early" cost?**
The use of the cash for the days it was paid early, at the Treasury's short-term funding rate of 9.5% a year. That's not the cost of capital used for long-term investment, which is higher; the what-if on the Paid early page shows how the cost moves with the rate.

**What counts as on time, and as late?**
Within five days either side of the due date is on time; more than five days after it is late. Days are counted from the invoice date, while the agreements count from receipt of the invoice, which is usually a few days later.

**Do the outcomes add up?**
Yes. Every payment reviewed is either taken with its discount, paid early, on time or paid late, and those four add up to the payments reviewed. A discount not taken is counted as well, alongside its timing.

**Can the figures be changed after approval?**
Not without it showing. The release is tied to a fingerprint of the approved figures and recommendations, and the record of every step has a tamper check.

**Does it pay or stop anything?**
No. It changes nothing in any system; it produces evidence for people to act on through their normal processes.

**How long does a review take?**
The review itself runs in minutes, and more agreements and payments take little longer, because the figures come from fixed rules. Connecting it to the organisation's own systems is something the Intellinexus team can talk through.

**Where do the results go?**
Into the project folder, and only once the release is approved: `03 Outputs` gets a folder named for the review, with the report, the management briefing and presentation and the one-page summary in `Report`, and the evidence pack, the audit trail and the data in `Evidence`; the record goes to `04 Records`. The folder syncs with OneDrive, so the team finds them there.

**What is the management pack?**
A management briefing (Word) and a presentation (PowerPoint) for the next meeting, filed with the release in the Sasol Design System. They use only the review's own figures, name the people who decided and approved, and close on how AI helped.

**Where can I see the data and the audit trail?**
In the dashboard report filed at release: its Data page shows every payment and every outcome row by row, and its Audit page every step and control, each with a download to Excel. The same workbooks are in the review's `Evidence` folder.

**Where can I see earlier reviews?**
In the project folder: each review has its own folder in `03 Outputs`, named for its review number, with its report, management briefing, presentation and one-page summary in `Report`, and its evidence in `Evidence`. I can answer from them, and the evidence is always the review's record in `04 Records`.

**Where do the recommendations come from?**
After the figures are calculated, I look at each supplier's pattern and recommend what to act on, with an owner. The review checks every figure I quote against its own before anything is shown, and the second person approves the recommendations with the findings.

**How is the projected gain worked out?**
By the review's fixed rules, not by me: each supplier's own cost over the period reviewed, carried forward 12 months, with each change phased in over its first three months. It assumes the next 12 months look like the period reviewed.

**Why isn't the whole discount counted as a gain?**
Taking a discount means paying sooner, and that costs the use of the cash. The rules count each discount net of that cost, at 9.5% a year, and don't offer the lever where the discount isn't worth it.

**Is the AI making the decisions?**
No. Claude reads, explains and recommends, fixed rules calculate every figure, and named people make every judgement and approval.
