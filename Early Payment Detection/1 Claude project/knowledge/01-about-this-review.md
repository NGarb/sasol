# About this review: Early Payment Detection

## The question it answers

*Are we paying suppliers on the right day, and taking the discounts we negotiated?* Paying before an invoice is due gives away the use of cash; paying after a discount window closes gives away the discount. Both hide in plain sight across many payments and agreements.

## What happens

1. The review reads the supplier agreements (Word) and the payment history (Excel) in the project folder, whatever they hold, and reports what it found: how many agreements and payments, their value excluding VAT, and the period the invoices cover.
2. Claude reads every agreement and records its payment period, settlement discount, termination notice and price-adjustment terms, quoting the clause each came from. An independent check reads every term a second way.
3. Where an agreement can be read two ways (an annexure that prevails, words and figures that disagree, an annexure only the supplier signed, a side letter that changes a discount, a period counted "from statement", an agreement that ended while payments continued, or a small enterprise the purchaser aims to pay sooner), the review **stops**, shows what each reading means for that agreement's payments, and asks a person to decide each one on a single card. The card starts on a recommended reading for each, with its reason; the person keeps or changes it, and gives their full name and their role.
4. Only then are the figures calculated, by fixed, repeatable rules: every payment is taken with its discount, paid early, on time or paid late, and the discounts not taken are counted alongside.
5. A different person, in a senior release role, approves the release, confirming they reviewed the findings, the decisions and the recommendations. Only then does the review file its results in the project folder: the dashboard report, a management briefing and a presentation for the next meeting, the one-page summary, the evidence pack, the audit trail and the data in `03 Outputs`, and a tamper-evident record in `04 Records`. Nothing is changed in any system.

## What makes it different

- **It knows when to stop.** An agreement that can be read two ways is a question for a person, not a guess, and each reading's effect is shown before anyone chooses.
- **Every term is quoted** in full from the clause it came from, and confirmed by an independent check.
- **It doesn't overclaim.** Discounts taken correctly are good practice, not findings; a payment within five days either side of its due date counts as on time; and paying a small enterprise early under an enterprise and supplier development commitment can be decided as intended.
- **Accountability is built in:** who decided what, in which role, why, and on exactly which figures, with a different person approving every release.

## The limits, stated plainly

- It covers the files in this project's folder, and it makes no claim about savings beyond them. Its figures are not group totals.
- It produces evidence for people to act on. It doesn't pay, hold or change anything.
- Paying early is valued at 9.5% a year, the Treasury's cost of short-term funding: a stated assumption, and not the cost of capital used for long-term investment decisions. The what-if on the Paid early page shows how the cost moves with the rate, without changing the approved figures.
