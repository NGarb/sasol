# Questions people ask

Answer in three sentences or fewer. Use this review's own results where they apply.

**Are these Sasol's own records?**
No. The review covers the files in this project's folder, which aren't Sasol's own records, so nothing here speaks to Sasol's own contracts.

**Why did it stop before counting anything?**
Because a few questions have two honest answers, and choosing between them is a business judgement, not a calculation: what counts as off contract, whether to rely on the contract register for agreements not in the folder, and whether to claim each rate rise. They're all on one card, decided by one person in a role allowed to make them, before anything is counted.

**Which definition is right?**
It's the decider's call. The review recommends counting only purchases in categories where a contract exists, because those are the ones that could have been bought on contract, and it records who decided and why.

**Why are agreements that aren't in the folder on the card?**
The purchases quote them, so the contract register says they exist, but they aren't in the project folder, so their terms can't be checked. Relying on the register counts those categories as contracted; otherwise their purchases without a reference aren't counted as off contract until the agreements are filed. Either way, a request for the signed agreements is drafted.

**How does it know when an agreement renews?**
It reads the term, the renewal period and the notice period in each agreement, and works out the last day to give notice for the period running on the review date: the day the review started, which stays the same for the whole review.

**What counts as a rate rise outside the terms?**
The agreement's rate clause allows a rise once a year from the anniversary, by no more than the published CPI for the second month before the notice, after the written notice it requires, delivered to the contracts office. A rise with no notice, with notice sent elsewhere, charged before the notice period ran or before the anniversary, or above CPI is outside the terms, valued invoice line by invoice line.

**Where does the CPI come from?**
Statistics South Africa's published headline CPI, annual change, for the month the agreement's rate clause names: for a notice given in October, August's figure. The escalation page shows the month and the figure used for each rise.

**What if we accept a rise?**
It's listed as accepted, and nothing is claimed. Only a rise the decider chose to claim becomes a rate query and a recovery; a referred one goes into the referral note for someone else to decide.

**Does it send the letters?**
No. It drafts the notices of non-renewal, the rate queries, the requests for the missing agreements and a referral note; a person reviews, signs and sends each one.

**Where do the results go?**
Into the project folder, and only once a second person approves: the files go to `03 Outputs`, in a folder named for the review with Report, Evidence and Actions inside, and the record to `04 Records`. The folder syncs with OneDrive, so the team finds them there. The files the review reads are never changed.

**What is in the management pack?**
A management briefing (Word) and a presentation (PowerPoint) in the Sasol Design System, filed with the release in the Report folder. They use only the review's own figures, and name who decided and who approved.

**Where can I see earlier reviews?**
In the project folder: each review has its own folder in `03 Outputs`, named for its review number, with its report, management briefing, presentation and one-page summary in `Report`, and its evidence in `Evidence`. I can answer from them, and the evidence is always the review's record in `04 Records`.

**Where do the recommendations come from?**
After the figures are calculated, I look at each agreement and category and recommend what to act on, with an owner. The review checks every figure I quote against its own before anything is shown, and the second person approves the recommendations with the findings.

**How is the projected gain worked out?**
By the review's fixed rules, not by me: an overcharge recovered once, an overcharge that stops once only the agreed rate is paid, or 5% of the spend moved onto contract, carried forward 12 months with each change phased in over its first three months. It's always shown in two parts: measured, and assumed at 5%.

**Where does the 5% come from?**
It's the review's planning assumption: buying on contract costs 5% less than buying off it. This review doesn't compare prices, so every value that rests on it says so.

**Why isn't a renewal counted as a gain?**
An agreement that renews on its current terms isn't a loss in itself: its spend is at stake, not lost. The review shows that spend as at stake and never adds it to the projected gain; what a better deal is worth is for the negotiation.

**Is the AI making the decisions?**
No. Claude reads the agreements and recommends what to act on, an independent check confirms every term, fixed rules do every calculation and value every recommendation, and named people make the decisions and approve the release.
