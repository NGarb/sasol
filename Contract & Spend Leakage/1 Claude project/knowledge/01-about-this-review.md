# About this review: Contract & Spend Leakage

## The question it answers

*We have contracts. Are we buying through them, and are their terms being honoured?* Value leaks out of contracts in quiet ways: purchases that bypass an agreement that exists, agreements that renew automatically because nobody gave notice in time, suppliers who raise their rates in ways the agreement doesn't allow, and agreements the purchases quote that nobody can find.

## What happens

1. The review reads the spend history, the panel agreements and the invoice lines in the project folder, whatever they hold, and reports what it found: how many orders, agreements and invoice lines, their value and the period they cover.
2. Claude reads every agreement's term, renewal, notice and rate-adjustment clauses, quoting each one, and an independent check confirms every term. The agreements differ, so each is read on its own terms.
3. **Before anything is counted, a person decides every judgement on one card:** what "off contract" means; whether to rely on the contract register for any agreement the purchases quote that isn't in the folder; and, for each rate rise outside its agreement's terms, whether to claim a credit note, accept it or refer it on. They give a reason for each, their full name and their role, from the roles allowed to decide.
4. Fixed rules then count off-contract spend under the definition decided, work out every notice deadline from the review date (the day the review starts), and check every invoice line against the agreement's rate terms: the anniversary, a valid notice to the contracts office, the notice period, and the published CPI for the month the terms name. The review drafts the notice letters, the rate queries for the rises claimed, the requests for the missing agreements and a referral note.
5. Claude then analyses each agreement and category and recommends what to act on, with an owner. The review checks every figure Claude quotes against its own, and values each recommendation with its own rules over the next 12 months, split into measured (credit notes and the agreed rate) and assumed (buying on contract at 5%).
6. A second person, in a senior role (Chief Procurement Officer, Financial Controller, Commercial Director or Chief Financial Officer), approves the release, confirming they reviewed the findings, the decisions and the recommendations. Only then does the review file everything in the project folder's `03 Outputs`: a report, a management briefing and a presentation, the evidence pack, an audit trail, the data, and the drafts; and a tamper-evident record in `04 Records`. Nothing is sent: every letter is a draft for a person to review, sign and send.

## What makes it different

- **It asks what you mean before it counts.** A confident, reasonable-sounding answer can count purchases no contract could have covered, and overstate the problem. The definition, the missing agreements and the rate rises are a person's decisions, recorded with their reasons and their name.
- **Every date and rand traces to a clause.** A notice deadline shows the renewal clause, quoted, and the calculation; a rate rise shows the rate clause, quoted, the CPI month it names, the notice and the invoices.
- **It ends in actions, not a chart.** The drafted letters are what a category manager would do on Monday morning.

## The limits, stated plainly

- It covers the files in this project's folder, over the period they cover, in rand excluding VAT, and it makes no claim beyond them. These are not group totals.
- The CPI figures are Statistics South Africa's published headline CPI, by month. The 5% by which buying on contract is taken to cost less is the review's planning assumption, shown wherever it's used; this review doesn't compare prices.
- It sends nothing, cancels nothing and changes nothing in any system.
