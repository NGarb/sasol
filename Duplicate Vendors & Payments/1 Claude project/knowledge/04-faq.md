# Questions people ask

Answer in three sentences or fewer. Use this review's own results where they apply.

**Are these Sasol's own records?**
No. The review covers the files in this project's folder, which aren't Sasol's own records, so nothing here speaks to Sasol's own suppliers.

**How does it find duplicates?**
Fixed rules compare every plausible pair on tax number, bank account, name and city, and score how strongly they match. Pairs at or above the policy line go to a person, with the evidence laid out field by field, including any orders for the same amount on both records.

**Why not just merge the obvious ones automatically?**
Because even "obvious" matches carry risk, and the person accountable should decide. The review recommends and shows its evidence, and a named person decides every pair.

**Who can decide, and who can approve?**
The pairs are decided by a Vendor Master Data Manager, Creditors Manager, Procurement Manager or Supplier Risk Manager, who gives their full name and a reason for each. The release is approved by a different person, as Financial Controller, Head of Internal Control, Chief Risk Officer or Chief Financial Officer, who confirms they reviewed the findings, the decisions and the recommendations.

**Why is a shared bank account not enough?**
A bank account says where money goes, not who owns it. Payment agents and group treasuries share accounts legitimately, so it's a question for forensic review, not a verdict. If a person decides the two are different suppliers, they confirm the bank details were verified, and that stays on both records.

**What does moving the threshold do?**
A higher line means fewer reviews but lets a shared bank account slip through; a lower one fills the queue with look-alike names. The Candidates page shows the trade-off live.

**How did it find the payments made twice?**
For every pair in the queue, the review looked across both records for orders of the same amount within 30 days, and showed them before anyone decided. They count as paid twice only once a person confirmed the two records are one supplier, and both orders were paid.

**What is a possible duplicate?**
The same match on a pair that was referred on, or decided as different suppliers. It's listed with its status, such as pending forensic review, and never counted as paid twice.

**Does it recover the money?**
No. It drafts the recovery letters and the change request; people in the responsible teams review and send them.

**Where do the results go?**
Into the project folder, and only once a second person approves: `03 Outputs`, in a folder named for the review, holds the report, the management briefing and the presentation, the evidence pack, the audit trail, the data and the drafts, and `04 Records` holds the record. The folder syncs with OneDrive, so the team finds them there. The files the review reads are never changed.

**What is the management pack?**
A management briefing (Word) and a presentation (PowerPoint) for the next meeting, in the Sasol Design System, filed with the release in the review's Report folder. They use only the review's own figures, and show the decisions with the names and roles of the people who made them.

**Where can I see earlier reviews?**
In the project folder: each review has its own folder in `03 Outputs`, named for its review number, with its report, management briefing, presentation and one-page summary in `Report`, and its evidence in `Evidence`. I can answer from them, and the evidence is always the review's record in `04 Records`.

**Where do the recommendations come from?**
After the figures are calculated, each pair's pattern is analysed and what to act on is recommended, with an owner. The review checks every figure in the analysis against its own before anything is shown, and the second person approves the recommendations with the findings.

**How is the projected gain worked out?**
By the review's fixed rules: an amount paid twice is recovered once, and merging the two records stops it recurring at the pace of the period reviewed, carried forward 12 months with the change phased in over its first three months. It assumes the next 12 months look like the period reviewed.

**Why isn't all the spend on the pairs counted as a gain?**
Only money paid twice can be recovered, or stopped from recurring. Where nothing was paid twice, the spend on a pair is shown as at stake, the risk the recommendations address, never as a saving.

**Is the AI making the decisions?**
No. The assessment and the analysis are there for people to weigh, fixed rules do the scoring, the counting and the valuing, and named people decide every pair and approve the release.
