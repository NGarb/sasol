# How decisions and approvals work in this review

Use this to answer questions about control, accountability and audit. Keep answers to three sentences.

## Who does what

| | Does | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Claude** | Reads the documents, quotes the clause behind every term, explains, and prepares everything for a decision, recommending an option for each with its reason and confidence | Decides what a contract means, or calculates a figure itself |
| **Fixed, repeatable calculations** | Compute every figure, the same way every time | Run before the decisions they depend on |
| **An independent check** | Reads every term a second way and flags any difference | Overrule a person |
| **The person deciding** | Makes each judgement, keeping or changing the recommendation and its reason, with their full name, in a role allowed to make it | Approves their own findings for release |
| **The approver** | A second person, in a role allowed to release this review, who confirms they reviewed what they approve | Made any decision in this review, or holds a role that decides |

## What a stop means

The review stops wherever the data can't settle a question on its own: for example, an agreement that states two different terms. Nothing that depends on the answer is calculated until a person decides. The card starts on the recommended option for each item, with its reason, and the person keeps it or changes it; nothing is recorded until they add their name and role and send it. The approval card starts on the recommendation for the release in the same way, and the approver still names themselves, chooses their role and ticks the confirmation themselves. "Refer it on" is always a valid answer: the item is set aside and listed, and the rest of the review continues.

The review stops in the same two places in every review: for the decisions, and for approval to release. Between them it carries on by itself.

## Who can decide, and who can approve

Each kind of decision can only be made by the roles below, under the delegation of authority for this review, and the release can only be approved by one of the release roles, which never overlap with the deciding roles. The person gives their full name and chooses their role on the card; the review refuses any other role, and refuses an approver who made a decision in this review.

| Decision | Roles that can make it |
|---|---|
| Supplier records | Vendor Master Data Manager, Creditors Manager, Procurement Manager, Supplier Risk Manager |
| Approval to release | Financial Controller, Head of Internal Control, Chief Risk Officer, Chief Financial Officer |

## What is recorded

The recommendation for each item (its option, reason and confidence) is recorded before anyone decides. Each decision records the option chosen, the reason (a sentence, not a word), whether it followed the recommendation and whether the reason is the person's own or the recommended one they kept, their full name and role, the time, and a fingerprint of the exact figures and recommendation they saw. The recommended reason can't be given for a different option. The release records the recommendation for it in the same way, and whether the approver followed it. The release records the approver's full name, role and reason, their confirmation that they reviewed the findings, the decisions and the recommendations, and fingerprints of the approved figures and recommendations. If either changed after approval, they can't be released without approving again. The audit trail filed with the results lists every step, decision and control, with who, when and why.

## The record of the review

Every step is kept in order, and each entry carries the fingerprint of the one before it. The tamper check confirms nothing was edited, removed or reordered; any change is detected and the entry named. It's tamper-*evident* within the review, not a certified ledger.

## What the review never does

It changes nothing in any system: no payment, block, merge or write-off. It produces evidence (a report, a management briefing and presentation, an evidence pack, an audit trail, the data, a one-page summary and the record) for people to act on through their normal processes.
